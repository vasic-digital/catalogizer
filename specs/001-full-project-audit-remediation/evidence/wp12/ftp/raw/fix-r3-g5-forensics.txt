===== 5. reading (what the traces show and what they do not) =====
* FACT (section 2, 3): a download closed early after `REST 1` (and after `REST 65536`) was NOT answered by pure-ftpd on the control channel within the whole wait (30 s with the committed client = IOTimeout, 3 s with the round-3 client = earlyCloseWait). A download closed early after `REST 0` was answered at once with "150 <statistics>".
* FACT: the PASS step of this server takes 1.7 to 3.1 s (a deliberate login delay), so every dropped connection costs a re-dial of that order against this server.
* FACT: the committed client waited the full IOTimeout (30 s in the fixture) at every such seek and then dropped the connection; two such seeks plus the 60 s test context gave the 65 s failure of section 1. In the author's round-2 evidence the same test took 36.64 s in one of three runs and 4.7 s in the other two: the stall depends on the server's behaviour at the moment of the close, and it was never analysed there.
* UNCONFIRMED: WHY pure-ftpd stays silent (a server-side behaviour on a TLS data connection closed with unread data; not investigated in its source). The client cannot make the server answer; it can only bound the wait, which round 3 does.
* The fix: `earlyCloseWait` (3 s) bounds the reply to an early-closed download; the seeker finishes a transfer whose SIZE bytes were all read AS COMPLETE (`finishAtEnd`) instead of closing it early; the integration test asserts that no seek step takes 15 s or more.
